Surfaces were fitted over SST 25–32 °C and chlorophyll-a 0.1–6 mg/m³ in the southwestern Bay of Bengal. The Gaussian and Lorentzian forms have centres far outside the SST domain, so within this window they behave as smooth monotonic functions of chlorophyll — that is expected, not a fitting error. Estimates outside the stated ranges, or in other basins, are not supported.

Pipeline

From pixel to biomass map

1 · Ocean colour products

SST and chlorophyll-a extracted from MODIS-Aqua and Oceansat-3 OCM-3 in SeaDAS, matched in space and time to cruise stations.

2 · In-situ pairing

Plankton biomass measured from WP-2 vertical hauls at each station across seven research cruises, giving the ground truth for the fit.

3 · Surface fitting

Four functional forms fitted and compared, with PBM feeding forward into the ZBM relationship to preserve the trophic link.

4 · Pixel-wise mapping

The chosen equation is written as a SeaDAS band-math expression and evaluated per pixel, producing basin-scale biomass maps.

Deployment

SeaDAS band math

Because every surface is closed-form arithmetic, it drops straight into SeaDAS as a band-math expression — no external model file, no Python runtime on the processing host.

Linear form, as entered in SeaDAS

PBM = 1.312 - (0.020 * sst) + (1.072 * chlor_a) ZBM = 1.201 + (0.693 * chlor_a) + (1.119 * PBM)

Substitute the band names used by your product — sst and chlor_a for MODIS-Aqua Level-2, or the OCM-3 equivalents. Evaluate PBM into its own band first, then reference it in the ZBM expression.
